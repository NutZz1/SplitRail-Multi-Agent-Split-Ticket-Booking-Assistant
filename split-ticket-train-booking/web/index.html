A THOUGHTFUL WAY TO COMPARE

One search. Four perspectives.

  1. Explore the same train

    A* searches real timetable edges and numbered seat inventory. Its heuristic is a relaxed-graph minimum remaining moving time. Find direct tickets and seat changes at eligible stops.

  2. Look for connections

    A second search explores journeys across different trains.

  3. Consider comfort and cost

    Two evaluators assess coach distance, night transfers, estimated fares, and journey time.

  4. Bring it all together

    The coordinator removes duplicates, checks constraints, and ranks options using a weighted score. You can sort and filter the results.

Recommended balances moving time, estimated fare, transfer penalty, and layover. It is not a guarantee of the cheapest possible fare. A ⚖ Pareto-optimal badge marks every option no other option beats on every one of fare, time, transfer comfort, and seat risk at once -- worth a look even when it isn't the one Recommended picked, since that single weighted trade-off isn't the only reasonable one. A ⚠ Degraded badge means one of the two comparison steps failed for that option and was filled in with a conservative estimate instead of stopping the whole search -- the option is still real, just scored with less certainty than the others. Same-train searches currently allow at most one coach change.

NO FINE PRINT

Real routes. Demo availability.

This academic prototype uses railway topology and schedules from datameet/railways. A curated set of trains support an actual reservation; the operational listing and recommendations use the same numbered seat inventory. Additional coach layouts and weekdays are explicitly simulated. All local Kerala station records, including corrected state metadata, are listed; stations missing halt times show data unavailable.
